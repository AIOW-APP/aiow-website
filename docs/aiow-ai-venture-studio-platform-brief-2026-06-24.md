# AIOW.ai — AI-driven Venture & Growth Partner

Date: 2026-06-24
Owner: Handsome / Team Richard
Status: strategic platform brief

## Verdict

This is the right strategic direction for AIOW.ai, with one correction: keep the public name **AIOW.ai** unless Richard intentionally rebrands. `ayo.ai` does not currently resolve from this machine, while `aiow.ai` is live. The concept should be expressed as:

> AIOW.ai is an AI-driven venture & growth partner. We invest technology, AI, automation, software, marketing, branding and digital growth into companies — and are rewarded through fees, revenue share, profit share, resale economics or equity depending on the opportunity.

AIOW should not position as a normal software agency. The strongest position is:

> Venture Studio + AI Agency + Software Company + Growth Partner + Investment Layer.

## Core thesis

Most entrepreneurs do not lack ideas. They lack:

- execution speed;
- AI/software capability;
- digital strategy;
- automation leverage;
- growth systems;
- conversion/branding;
- data discipline;
- reliable operators.

AIOW can become the AI-native partner that turns a founder/company's market access, domain knowledge and ambition into a digital growth machine.

## Commercial models

AIOW should support combinations of:

1. Fixed project fee
2. Monthly growth retainer
3. Revenue share
4. Profit share
5. Resale / white-label share
6. Module/usage fees
7. Equity / warrants / options
8. Hybrid models

Baseline rule already adopted:

- minimum 10% revenue share where AIOW materially contributes to growth;
- minimum 10% resale/doorverkoop/white-label share;
- higher percentages when AIOW provides core IP, platform, automation, strategy, distribution or takes more risk.

## Scoring model

| Score | Meaning | Key signals |
|---|---|---|
| Founder Score | Can this person/team execute? | experience, time, contacts, prior execution, budget, motivation |
| Market Score | Is the market attractive? | size, pain, willingness to pay, trend, competition, timing |
| Execution Score | Can this be delivered and sold? | clarity, owner, sales path, ops capacity, proof, roadmap |
| AI Opportunity Score | Can AIOW create unfair advantage? | automation leverage, data, workflows, model fit, repeatability |
| Investment Score | Should AIOW take upside risk? | total expected return vs effort/risk/IP/strategic value |

Recommended verdicts:

- **REJECT** — too vague, no proof, bad founder fit, unattractive market, or high legal/risk.
- **PAID PROJECT** — useful work but not enough upside/risk quality for revshare/equity.
- **GROWTH PARTNER** — monthly fee + KPI roadmap.
- **REVENUE SHARE** — AIOW can directly drive revenue lift.
- **PROFIT SHARE** — profit data is reliable and AIOW impacts margins/growth.
- **EQUITY/HYBRID** — large upside, strong founder/market, AIOW builds core asset or platform.

## Deal model heuristics

### Paid project

Use when:

- opportunity is useful but not venture-grade;
- client has budget;
- AIOW effort is bounded;
- upside is uncertain.

### Growth partner retainer

Use when:

- existing company has revenue;
- AIOW can improve marketing/automation/software;
- monthly KPI work is measurable.

### Revenue share

Use when:

- AIOW can trace impact to revenue;
- payments/CRM can be measured;
- growth upside is material.

Baseline: 10%; strong cases 15-30%; strategic cases 25-40%.

### Profit share

Use when:

- margins are reliable;
- AIOW improves profit or reduces cost;
- bookkeeping is transparent.

Requires financial data access and anti-gaming clauses.

### Equity / hybrid

Use when:

- AIOW builds core platform/IP;
- founder has market/distribution edge;
- upside is large;
- cash budget is limited but opportunity is strong.

Requires legal documents, governance, vesting/milestones and exit rules.

## Legal/risk notes

AIOW must be careful with language:

- do not imply regulated financial advice;
- equity/profit-share deals need legal review;
- revenue/profit definitions must be contractually precise;
- data access and privacy must be scoped;
- AI recommendations must be advisory, not guaranteed outcomes;
- conflicts of interest and exclusivity need explicit handling;
- investment/participation structures may trigger regulatory/tax/legal implications depending on jurisdiction.
